# Hypothesis & Success Metrics (Module 3)

## Pre-work · Hypothesis check
- **Role , who you are solving for (from M2):** A financially stable heavy BNPL user who runs Klarna, PayPal and Riverty at the same time.
- **Goal , what this user is ultimately trying to achieve:** To keep the control that made her choose BNPL by seeing everything she owes in one place.
- **Friction / moment of misery , the specific pain blocking their goal:** At month-end she opens three apps and copies amounts into a spreadsheet, because Riverty card spend takes up to 24 hours to appear, due dates sit on two screens, and some charges show a processor name instead of the merchant. A repayment notification arrived after the due date, so she paid a reminder fee. "I'm not broke, I just can't see everything in one place."
- **Current workaround , the external tool or manual process they rely on (M2):** A self-built spreadsheet with three columns: what, how much, which date. Manual copying between the Klarna, PayPal and Riverty apps. Memory and matching by amount to identify charges.
- **Problem Hook , your one-sentence framing of the business crisis (M1):** Riverty's users juggle several BNPL apps and pay at the till with someone else's card, while Klarna and PayPal grow into the stores and checkouts Riverty can't reach, and with a brand preference of only 6%, every month without a card moves Riverty closer to being a replaceable checkout button.
- **Value Proposition , the outcome your initiative promised to deliver (M1):** For busy BNPL users like Lena, who want money to leave their account only after they've decided to keep what they bought, the Riverty card lets them pay later anywhere, in stores and online, with one clear view of what's due. Unlike Klarna's debit-first card or PayPal's separate instalment products, it keeps the "decide first, pay after" habit in every setting and sends users back to Riverty's partner merchants instead of away from them. New value: Riverty moves from a checkout option Lena doesn't prefer to an everyday card she carries. That closes the in-store and heavy-user gaps from the brief and gives Riverty a direct consumer relationship for the first time. Why now: Klarna's card base is growing, and PayPal now offers instalments at Amazon.de and a virtual card for in-store payments, so Lena's habits are being set by competitors. From 20 November 2026, most BNPL falls under consumer credit rules, and Riverty carries that compliance cost with or without a card. Riverty's banking licence and Mastercard platform are already in place, and a consumer card is the next product that can earn that investment. The merchant benefit is a hypothesis, not something the brief establishes, so it should be one of the first things tested in discovery.

- **Quantitative evidence (from M3) , name the metric or data point that confirms the pain; cite the number:** 1. “I can’t see everything in one place” / opening multiple apps - Many interviewees already use budget apps or books to compensate: “Keeps a budget book,” “Uses budget app Monkey,” “finanzguru,” and “Currently using an app for it” (Spending Limit).

- **Persona , role, goal, and the friction you confirmed in the reconciliation steps:** Persona: Lena, 32, Cologne

Source: Lena comes from the case study brief, not the interviews. The interview evidence below supports her profile but doesn’t prove it. The 15 participants were German Riverty users, and none of them used a live Riverty card.

Role: Salaried employee on a middling income with no real cushion. She is a mobile-first shopper, uses BNPL on most non-trivial purchases, and has Klarna and PayPal installed. At month-end she acts as her own finance clerk, reconciling card spend, invoices and due dates by hand.

Goal: Know what she owes, to whom and by when, from one place, and keep money in her account until she has decided to keep the item. The interviews back this up. Participants cite returns, receiving the item before paying, and not wanting to forget a payment as reasons for invoice and BNPL use. Her real job-to-be-done is control and certainty, not credit.

- **Problem you are solving , one sentence describing the specific friction this initiative removes:** Riverty card users like Lena can’t see all their card spend, due dates and merchant details in one real-time place, so they rebuild the picture by hand at month-end and risk missing a payment and paying a reminder fee.
- **Strategic outcome , what behaviour change do you expect, and how does it map to retention / revenue / churn?:** Riverty becomes the place where Lena sees and settles her card spend, so the card becomes a habit rather than one more option at checkout. That raises engagement with existing heavy users, which is the strategic goal in the pitch deck, rather than short-term unit economics.

The pitch is explicit that the card has weak unit economics (CM falls from 1.3% to about -0.1% once merchant fees are replaced by interchange). The justification is Customer Base Value: active users, actions, customer lifetime and growth in usage. So the outcome should be framed as engagement and retention first, with revenue following indirectly.

Caveats
Reminder fees cut both ways. Helping Lena avoid them improves trust and retention, but it may reduce fee income. Decide whether fees are a revenue line you plan on or a pain you remove.
Only 5% signed up in the fake door test. That is a weak sign-up signal, and it came from app users only.
Heavy app users are the target. The pitch estimates about 82k of them, and the case model’s 5% conversion applies to a much larger base. Testing that assumption would be a good first step.
Several conditions must hold. Interviewees named security, trust in Riverty, no SCHUFA impact, and acceptance everywhere as preconditions. Retention gains depend on these.
Parts of the overview arrive later. The MVP is a basic overview, with categorisation and open-banking overview in later iterations. The strongest retention lever therefore lands after launch.
Leading indicators
Habit: share of active users opening the overview at least weekly.
Share of wallet: card transactions per active user per month.
Payment behaviour: on-time payment rate and share of users on automatic payment.
Retention: 12-month churn of card users against the 5% assumption and against non-card heavy users.
Revenue: Flex conversion and interchange per active card.
- **Primary success metric (initiative signal) , the leading indicator that tells you the gap is closing:** Share of active card users who open the Riverty overview at least weekly (weekly overview rate).
- **Guardrail metric (product signal) , the metric that must NOT drop; it protects your existing base:** Existing-BNPL volume per heavy user (Riverty Pay GMV at partner merchants, among users who activated the card).
- **Decision window , how much time or data before you scale, pivot, or kill? minimum threshold to proceed?:** Recommendation: make three gated decisions rather than one scale/pivot/kill call. Each gate needs at least two full billing cycles of data, because the pain you’re solving (due dates, month-end reconciliation, reminder fees) only shows up across a cycle.
